# Campus Cafe: A Measured Launch

**Decision requested:** Approve a limited operating pilot, with continuation tied to observed sales and costs. The break-even model below is a planning case, not a demand forecast.

## What the evidence says

Student food-spending evidence is not yet campus-specific. The Hope Center's 2023-24 survey report found food insecurity among 41% of respondents across 91 participating colleges in 16 states; this identifies affordability pressure in that survey sample, not cafe demand at this university. Source: [The Hope Center, 2023-2024 Student Basic Needs Survey Report](https://hope.temple.edu/research/hope-center-basic-needs-survey/2023-2024-student-basic-needs-survey-report). The Kaggle file included with this project has 1,000 rows, but its publisher explicitly says the records are fictional. It is excluded from demand estimates: [Kaggle dataset and description](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/sumanthnimmagadda/student-spending-dataset).

For a price reference, Toast reported average U.S. prices of $3.08 for regular coffee and $5.46 for lattes in Q1 2024, based on Toast platform data. This is a dated platform benchmark, not a representative survey or a local campus menu. Source: [Toast, Coffee and Tea Trends in All 50 States](https://pos.toasttab.com/news/most-popular-coffee-and-tea-drinks-toast-restaurant-trends-report). Use the following **test prices, all assumptions rather than surveyed local prices**: brewed coffee $3, espresso drink $5, and pastry $4. The $8 average transaction below assumes a beverage-led mix with some food add-ons. Check nearby menus and willingness to pay before setting permanent prices.

The National Restaurant Association's 2026 culinary forecast highlights health and value as menu considerations. This is national industry context, not evidence of local preferences; test a plant-forward option and lower-waste service rather than promising demand or environmental savings. Source: [National Restaurant Association, 2026 Culinary Forecast](https://restaurant.org/education-and-resources/resource-library/what%E2%80%99s-hot-in-2026-comfort-health-and-value/). Track unit sales, unsold food, and packaging during the pilot; make no emissions claim without product- and waste-specific evidence.

## Monthly break-even case

**Assumptions:** 8,000 enrolled students (scenario input); $24,000 fixed monthly costs ($17,500 staffing including payroll load, $4,500 facilities/utilities, $2,000 maintenance and administration); $8.00 average transaction; food and packaging at 35% of sales; card fees at 3% of sales; and 22 open days per month. These costs and prices are planning assumptions, not vendor quotes or observed campus data.

- Variable cost per transaction: $8.00 x (35% + 3%) = **$3.04**.
- Contribution per transaction: $8.00 - $3.04 = **$4.96**.
- Monthly break-even volume: $24,000 / $4.96 = **4,838.71**, rounded up to **4,839 transactions**.
- Daily pace: 4,839 / 22 = **219.95**, or about **220 transactions per open day**.

This excludes construction, equipment, financing, and any costs not listed above. It assumes every sale has the same average ticket and margin; actual mix, staffing, and supplier prices will change the threshold.

## Recommendation and controls

Run a time-limited pilot before committing to a permanent build-out. Validate prices with local menus, survey students about likely purchase frequency, and report weekly transactions, average ticket, labor, food cost, and waste against the assumptions above. Continue only if measured contribution and demand support the full operating cost. The current evidence does not establish that 220 daily transactions are achievable.
